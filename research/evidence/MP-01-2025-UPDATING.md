# MP-01 — Post-2024 Updating Evidence: 2025

## Purpose

Test whether the policy process continued to update its diagnosis after the 2024 tightening sequence.

This is **post-intervention evidence**. It cannot be treated as evidence that was available when the 2024 decisions were made.

## Observed updating

The CBN's 2025 MPC activity report states that the Committee maintained a tight stance through February, May and July 2025, then reduced the MPR by 50 basis points in September after sustained disinflation over five consecutive months. In November it retained the September settings while noting continued inflation deceleration.

The report describes the 2025 process as repeated assessment of domestic and global developments, with attention to inflation, exchange rates, liquidity, external conditions and structural constraints. citeturn3search0

### What changed

- February 2025: MPR remained 27.50%.
- May 2025: MPR remained 27.50%.
- July 2025: MPR remained 27.50%.
- September 2025: MPR reduced to 27.00%; commercial-bank CRR reduced from 50% to 45%; standing-facilities corridor recalibrated.
- November 2025: MPR remained 27.00%, with further corridor adjustment.

The September change was explicitly tied in the CBN's retrospective account to sustained disinflation and improved macroeconomic fundamentals. citeturn3search0

## Interpretation for the transfer audit

This evidence weakens any simplistic hypothesis that a later policy process simply repeats a historical instrument without updating.

The observed sequence contains:

> tightening → observation → continued tightening while uncertainty remained → sustained disinflation → partial easing

That is an actual adaptive sequence.

However, this does **not** answer the deeper question.

The remaining test is whether the process updated only in response to observed outcomes, or whether it also explicitly reassessed the **structural validity of the transmission mechanism**.

Those are different operations.

### Outcome updating

Evidence:
- inflation fell;
- exchange-rate conditions improved;
- food-price pressures moderated;
- financial conditions changed.

The CBN attributes the improvement to a combination of sustained monetary tightening, exchange-rate stability and improved agricultural production rather than to MPR alone. citeturn3search0

### Mechanism updating

Still unresolved:

- Did the MPC estimate or explicitly reassess MPR pass-through?
- Did it distinguish the effect of MPR from CRR/liquidity operations?
- Did it reassess exchange-rate pass-through after the 2023 FX regime change?
- Did it change its confidence in specific transmission channels?
- Were alternative mechanisms formally compared?

## Important methodological result

The empirical question is becoming narrower and more useful.

We can already observe **evidence-informed policy updating** in the 2024–2025 sequence.

Therefore the research should not ask whether policymakers update.

Instead ask:

> **What exactly is being updated: the observed outcome, the diagnosis, the estimated causal mechanism, or the structural conditions required for that mechanism?**

An outcome-based update and a mechanism-validity update are not equivalent.

## Next test

Reconstruct the CBN's own monetary-transmission evidence and models available during 2023–2025.

Specifically seek evidence on:

1. interest-rate pass-through;
2. credit channel;
3. expectations channel;
4. exchange-rate channel;
5. liquidity channel;
6. structural/regime changes affecting those channels.

Then compare those estimates with the 2011 evidence base.

Only after that comparison can we determine whether the proposed transfer audit adds an operation not already present in monetary-policy evaluation.

## Current case status

**Unclassified.**

Evidence now establishes:
- contemporaneous decision reasoning existed;
- 2024 decisions responded to changing observations;
- post-2024 policy subsequently changed after sustained disinflation;
- multiple causal mechanisms and structural conditions were recognized.

Evidence does not yet establish:
- that the 2024 mechanism was simply transferred from 2011;
- that the mechanism was structurally invalid;
- that monetary tightening caused the observed disinflation;
- that the CBN failed to validate its mechanism.
