# MP-01 — 2024 Meeting-Level Decision Evidence

## Purpose

This artifact records what the CBN's own contemporaneous documents say about the 2024 tightening sequence. It deliberately separates:

- **decision evidence**: what the MPC said before/at the decision;
- **mechanism interpretation**: what mechanism the statement appears to rely on;
- **later evidence**: evidence published after the decision that must not be retroactively attributed to the MPC.

No causal conclusion is made here.

## What is now directly established

### 1. The 2024 sequence was explicitly data- and diagnosis-driven

The CBN record does not support a description of the 2024 tightening sequence as unexplained repetition. The February member statements explicitly reference inflation data, exchange-rate depreciation/pass-through, money supply and structural factors. The May statement explicitly evaluates month-on-month inflation changes as evidence about the effect of the tightening stance. The July statement acknowledges an inflation uptick while maintaining the view that monetary policy would gain further traction.

### 2. The policy was not a pure MPR intervention

CRR changes and corridor changes accompanied major decisions. Therefore an observed post-policy response cannot simply be attributed to the MPR without separating the components of the intervention.

### 3. The MPC distinguished at least some monetary and non-monetary mechanisms

Food inflation remained a recurring concern, while exchange-rate pass-through and fiscal measures also appeared in the decision record. This matters because an inflation response is not automatically evidence that the interest-rate channel caused the response.

### 4. Later evidence must remain separate

The IMF's April/May 2024 Article IV analysis estimated that tighter financial conditions were associated with lower inflation after controlling for exchange rates, and projected further effects over subsequent quarters. That is contemporaneous external analytical evidence, but it is not evidence that the CBN necessarily used that exact model in making its February or March decision.

## Important unresolved question

The reconstruction now gives us a sharper test:

> **Did the 2024 decision process merely observe that inflation was high and tighten, or did it explicitly establish that the relevant transmission mechanism was operating under the changed post-2023 exchange-rate and financial structure?**

There is evidence for the first proposition: the MPC clearly diagnosed high inflation and related pressures.

The second proposition requires more work.

## Next evidence required

1. Locate pre-2024 Nigerian estimates of monetary-policy transmission that were available to the MPC.
2. Identify CBN's own transmission analysis/model evidence, not only external IMF analysis.
3. Compare the mechanism assumptions in 2011 with those documented in 2024.
4. Examine whether the 2024 MPC explicitly discussed the changed FX regime as changing transmission.
5. Trace 2025 decisions for evidence of subsequent updating after the full 2024 tightening sequence.
6. Separate the effect of MPR, CRR, OMO/liquidity management, FX changes and supply/fiscal measures in the outcome analysis.

## Evidence boundary

This artifact supports a stronger empirical statement than the original hypothesis:

> **The 2024 CBN record provides observable evidence of iterative diagnosis and policy adjustment. Whether that process validated the continued operation of the relevant transmission mechanism under changed structural conditions remains unresolved.**

That is the question to test next.
